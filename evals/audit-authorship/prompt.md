Why Your Team Should Write Down Its Decisions

Every team makes hundreds of decisions each quarter. Which vendor to choose, how to structure a database, whether to delay a launch for one more round of testing. Most of these choices are made in meetings or chat threads, and within a few months almost nobody can say exactly why they were made. The people who were in the room move on, memories fade, and the reasoning quietly disappears.

That loss is expensive. New hires spend weeks reverse-engineering choices that seem strange without context. Old debates get reopened because no one remembers they were already settled. Worse, teams sometimes undo a good decision simply because its original constraints were forgotten.

Writing decisions down fixes much of this. A short record that captures the question, the options considered, and the reasoning behind the final call takes about fifteen minutes to produce. In this post, we'll look at what a useful decision record contains, how to keep the habit light enough that people actually follow it, and what we've learned from doing it ourselves.
