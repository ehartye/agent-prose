# Diffusion: Random Motion, Predictable Change

Imagine adding a drop of food coloring to a glass of water without stirring. At first, most coloring particles are crowded near the drop. Over time, they spread through the water. This spreading illustrates **diffusion**.

Particles in fluids—liquids and gases—are constantly moving in random directions. They do not know where other particles are, and they do not choose to move toward an empty space. Yet their random movements can produce a predictable overall change.

## Concentration and net movement

**Concentration** describes how much of a substance occupies a given space. A **concentration gradient** is a difference in concentration from one location to another.

Suppose coloring particles are more concentrated on one side of a container. Particles cross between the two sides in both directions. Because more coloring particles start on the crowded side, more tend to cross from that side during the same time interval. The result is **net movement** from higher concentration to lower concentration: diffusion down the concentration gradient.

“Net” means the overall result after movement in opposite directions is taken into account. Individual particles can still move up the gradient.

## What happens at equilibrium?

As diffusion continues, the concentration difference becomes smaller. Eventually, the coloring is evenly distributed. The system has reached **dynamic equilibrium**.

The word “dynamic” matters: particles keep moving and crossing between regions. Over time, movement in one direction balances movement in the other, so there is no net flow. Equilibrium does not mean that particles have stopped.

## How does the gradient affect speed?

A steeper concentration gradient means concentration changes more sharply across a distance. Under otherwise identical conditions, a steeper gradient produces a faster rate of net diffusion. As the gradient becomes less steep, net diffusion slows.

However, the gradient is not the only factor that matters. Temperature, the distance particles must travel, and the medium they travel through also affect diffusion rate. To compare the effect of gradients fairly, keep those other conditions the same.
