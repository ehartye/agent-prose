# Diffusion: Random Motion, Predictable Results

Imagine placing a drop of dye in water. At first, dye particles are crowded near the drop. Over time, they spread through the water. This spreading can happen without stirring because particles in fluids, including liquids and gases, are constantly moving in random directions.

**Diffusion** is the net movement of particles from an area of higher concentration toward an area of lower concentration, caused by this random motion. Concentration means the amount of a substance in a given volume. A **concentration gradient** is a change in concentration over distance.

## How random motion produces net movement

Dye particles do not sense empty space or decide where to go. Each particle moves randomly, whatever the surrounding concentration. Some particles move toward an area with fewer dye particles; others move toward an area with more.

Why does the dye spread, then? Imagine a boundary between a crowded area and a less crowded area. More particles are available to cross from the crowded side. Over a short time, more particles cross toward the less crowded side than cross back. The difference between these two movements is the **net movement**.

For example, if 100 particles cross one way and 40 cross back, the net movement is 60 particles in the first direction. Particles moved both ways, even though the overall result favors one direction.

## What makes diffusion faster?

A steeper concentration gradient means concentration changes more sharply over a given distance. If all other conditions are identical, a steeper gradient produces a faster net rate of diffusion. As particles spread out, the gradient becomes less steep, so the net movement slows.

The gradient is not the only factor. Temperature affects particle motion; higher temperatures generally increase diffusion rates. Distance matters because diffusion over a longer distance takes more time. The medium—the material particles move through—also affects how easily they spread. Comparing two diffusion rates therefore requires considering these conditions as well as the gradient.

## Does diffusion stop at equilibrium?

Eventually, concentration may become equal throughout the connected space. This is **dynamic equilibrium**. “Dynamic” means that particles are still moving. They continue crossing between areas, but equal amounts cross in opposite directions on average. There is no net flow, even though individual particles never stop moving.

## Practice

1. Two connected chambers contain the same gas at different concentrations. In which direction is its net movement? Are particles also moving the other way?
2. Two otherwise identical setups have different concentration gradients. Which has the faster net diffusion rate?
3. At equilibrium, have the particles stopped? Explain.

**Check your answers:** 1. From higher to lower concentration; yes. 2. The setup with the steeper gradient. 3. No. Random movement continues, with no net flow.
