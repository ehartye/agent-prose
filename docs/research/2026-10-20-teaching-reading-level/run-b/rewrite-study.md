Students who said they slept at least eight hours on school nights also reported higher average grades. This link remained after researchers accounted for students’ ages and schools.

The study does not show that more sleep caused better grades. Researchers did not measure family resources, existing health conditions, or study habits. These factors could help explain the link. Both sleep and grades were self-reported. The results come from participating schools and cannot automatically be applied to all teenagers.

A study that randomly assigns students to a program intended to improve sleep would address a different question: whether assigning that particular program changes grades. Its conclusions would still depend on how students were assigned, whether they followed the program, and how researchers measured the results.
