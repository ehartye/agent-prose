# What the sleep study can tell us

In this fictional study, students who reported sleeping at least eight hours on school nights also reported higher average grades. This association—a link between two things—remained after researchers accounted for age and school.

The study does not establish that more sleep causes higher grades. Researchers did not measure family resources, health before the study, or study habits. These factors could help explain the link. Sleep and grades were also reported by students themselves. The findings apply to the participating schools; they do not establish the same pattern for all adolescents. No sample size or size of the grade difference was supplied.

A randomized intervention would ask a different causal question: what happens when students are randomly assigned to an effort to change sleep? It would not simply repeat this comparison of reported sleep and grades. How students are assigned, whether they follow the assigned intervention, and how outcomes are measured would still matter.
