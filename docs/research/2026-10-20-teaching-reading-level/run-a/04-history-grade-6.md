# When accounts of the past disagree

Two people who saw the same event can tell different stories without lying. They may have watched from different places or at different times. They may tell the story for different reasons or remember different details. An account is **contemporaneous** if it comes from the time of the event. Even then, it may contain mistakes.

Historians use **corroboration** to check accounts. This means comparing independent accounts to see where they agree or disagree. Independent accounts do not copy the same report. For example, five books that copy one person's story still give us only that person's account.

When accounts disagree, ask what each person could have seen and when. Why did the person tell the story? Then look for support from other independent accounts. Agreement can help, but copied stories add no new evidence. Some conflicts may remain. If the evidence does not settle them, explain what we still do not know.

This is a fictional exercise. We have no real accounts or quotes to study, so we cannot judge any one witness here.
