# Weather: what we observe and what we predict

An **observation** tells what someone sees or measures. It can tell about weather now or at an earlier time. A **prediction** tells what someone thinks will happen later. That weather has not happened yet. No one has observed it.

Here is an invented example. Maya looks outside and sees rain falling. That is an observation. She reads a thermometer and writes down the temperature. That is an observation, too. Her record of yesterday's temperature is still an observation, even though she made it earlier.

Then Maya hears a forecast saying that rain may stop in the afternoon. A forecast is a weather prediction. It describes weather that has not been observed yet. Maya cannot check the afternoon weather while it is still morning.

Forecasters use observations to make predictions. They also use models, which help them work out how weather may change. A model does not show the future for certain. Forecasts remain uncertain and can be wrong.

Even so, a forecast can help Maya plan. If rain is predicted, she may take a raincoat. Later, she can observe what happens and compare it with the forecast. An observation records weather; a prediction helps us prepare for weather still ahead.
