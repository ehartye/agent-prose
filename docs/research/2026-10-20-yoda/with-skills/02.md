# Classroom coaching pilot

In this fictional pilot, students who finished the quiz averaged 12 out of 20 points with coaching and 11 in the comparison group. These averages do not show that coaching caused better scores.

One school invited 60 eligible student volunteers, and 48 consented, meaning they agreed to participate. Researchers randomly assigned 24 students to coaching and 24 to the comparison group. Six coaching students and four comparison students did not finish the final quiz. This loss of participants is called **attrition**.

The averages cover only the 18 coaching students and 20 comparison students who finished. The notes give no outcomes for the others. Random assignment does not resolve those missing outcomes or make this difference a demonstrated coaching effect.

The notes also give no measures of how widely scores varied and no statistical tests. Tutors knew each student’s group. The shared quiz was graded without hiding students’ groups from graders.

Six volunteer interviewees said they felt more confident. Their reports do not measure a confidence effect. Because the pilot involved one school, these records alone do not establish results for other schools.

Source: Harbor Learning Lab (2026), classroom pilot notes (fictional).
