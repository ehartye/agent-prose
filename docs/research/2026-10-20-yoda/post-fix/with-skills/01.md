# Conditional probability: Which group counts?

After this lesson, you should be able to explain why the word **given** can change a probability.

Consider a fictional club with exactly 100 members. Of these, 40 play a musical instrument, 60 play a sport, and 30 do both. Select one member at random, with every member equally likely to be chosen.

First ask: What is the probability that the member does both activities? All 100 members are possible choices, and 30 do both. The probability is 30/100 = 3/10. The denominator, the fraction's bottom number, counts the whole group being considered.

## How “given” changes the group

**Conditional probability** is the probability of something happening when a condition is already known to hold. The word **given** introduces that condition and limits which members we count.

Given that the member plays a sport, what is the probability they play an instrument? Consider only the 60 sport players. Among them, 30 also play an instrument, so the probability is 30/60 = 1/2.

Now reverse the condition. Given that the member plays an instrument, what is the probability they play a sport? Consider only the 40 instrument players. Of these, 30 also play a sport, so the probability is 30/40 = 3/4.

The same 30 members appear in all three numerators. The denominators differ because the questions allow different groups: everyone, sport players, or instrument players. Always identify the given group before forming the fraction.

These numbers describe only this fictional club. They do not show that either activity causes the other. Use the counts rather than assuming that knowing one activity leaves the chance of the other unchanged.

## Practice

Given that a member plays an instrument, what is the probability they do **not** play a sport?

## Answer key

Of the 40 instrument players, 40 − 30 = 10 do not play a sport. The probability is 10/40 = 1/4.
