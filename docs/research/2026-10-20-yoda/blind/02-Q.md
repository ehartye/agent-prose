# A Classroom Coaching Pilot

At one school, researchers invited 60 eligible student volunteers to join a study, and 48 agreed. They randomly assigned 24 students to coaching and 24 to a comparison group.

Six students in coaching and four in the comparison group did not finish the final quiz. This loss of participants before the final measurement is called **attrition**. The 18 coaching students who finished averaged 12 out of 20 points. The 20 comparison students who finished averaged 11 out of 20.

That one-point difference does not demonstrate that coaching caused higher scores. Although assignment was random, the averages leave out students who did not finish. Their quiz outcomes were not provided. The notes also give no information about how widely scores varied or statistical tests of the difference. Tutors knew students’ group assignments, and graders of the shared quiz also knew them, which could have influenced results.

Six volunteer interviewees said they felt more confident. Their reports do not measure how much coaching changed confidence. Because the pilot involved only one school, these records do not establish what would happen at other schools.

Fictional source: Harbor Learning Lab (2026), classroom pilot notes.
