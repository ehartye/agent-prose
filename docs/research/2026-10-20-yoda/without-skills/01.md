# Conditional Probability: Counting Within a Group

Imagine a fictional club with 100 members. You choose one member at random, so everyone has the same chance of being selected. Of the members, 40 play a musical instrument, 60 play a sport, and 30 do both.

**Conditional probability** is the chance that something is true when you already know another fact. The word **given** tells you which group to count within. That group supplies the denominator, the total number of members considered in your fraction.

First, ask for the probability that a member plays an instrument, **given that the member plays a sport**. We count only the 60 sport players. Among them, 30 also play an instrument, so the probability is **30/60 = 1/2**.

Now reverse the condition: Find the probability that a member plays a sport, **given that the member plays an instrument**. This time, we count only the 40 instrument players. The same 30 members do both, so the probability is **30/40 = 3/4**.

What if we simply ask for the probability that a randomly selected member does both? There is no condition limiting the group. We count all 100 members, giving **30/100 = 3/10**.

All three numerators are 30, but the denominators differ. Before calculating, ask: “Which members can I count?” Reversing the condition can change the answer because it changes that group. These numbers describe participation; they do not show that one activity causes the other.

## Practice

A selected member plays a sport. What is the probability that the member does **not** play an instrument?

## Answer

Of the 60 sport players, 30 play an instrument, leaving 30 who do not. The probability is **30/60 = 1/2**.
