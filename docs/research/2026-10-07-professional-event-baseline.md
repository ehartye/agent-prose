**Task A — 45-minute customer webinar production package**

**Working title:** Reduce Handoff Delays: Clear Ownership, Better Inputs, Faster Follow-Through

**Audience:** Operations directors responsible for work that moves between teams.

**Audience promise:** Attendees will leave with a practical way to identify a troublesome handoff, clarify responsibility, and measure whether a change helps.

**Production acceptance criteria:**

- Finish within 45 minutes, including questions.
- Demonstrate one complete handoff scenario.
- Have a usable backup for every live demo step.
- Give attendees a handoff checklist and measurement worksheet.
- Make no unsupported performance claims.

The “30% improvement” claim is not ready for customer use. Keep it out of slides, narration, promotional copy, and prepared answers until supporting evidence establishes what improved, against which baseline, over what period, and for which customers. A safe replacement is: “We’ll show a process for reducing avoidable waiting and measuring the result.”

| Production | Prepare demo environment and backup assets | Own meeting setup, admission, recording, timing, polls, and questions |
| Rehearsal | Run the complete presentation and demo | Run opening, cues, interruptions, and recovery |
| Live session | Present and demonstrate | Monitor time and attendee experience; select questions |
| Follow-up | Verify substantive answers and resources | Assemble resource links and unanswered questions |

With two people, the moderator has several simultaneous duties. Keep engagement simple: one poll, questions submitted throughout, and a dedicated question period.

**Slide and content brief**

1. **Title and audience promise.** Show the three takeaways.
2. **Where handoffs lose time.** Simple flow: ready to transfer → waiting for an owner → clarification → accepted → work starts. Explain that waiting time and active work time are different.
3. **Opening poll.** “Where does your work most often stall?” Choices: unclear owner; incomplete information; competing priorities; exceptions that need approval.
4. **A workable handoff agreement.** Identify sender, receiver, required information, acceptance signal, expected response window, and exception owner.
5. **An example agreement.** Use a clearly labeled fictional example relevant to the product: an operations request moving from intake to fulfillment.
6. **What to measure.** Handoff waiting time, first-pass acceptance, clarification loops, and unresolved backlog. Include an end-to-end measure to detect delay shifted elsewhere.
7. **Demo scenario.** Explain the sample request, roles, and expected visible outcome.
8. **First experiment.** Choose one recurring handoff, record a baseline, change a small number of rules, and compare results.
9. **Takeaway worksheet.** Show where attendees can fill in their own workflow.
10. **Recap and resource link.** Repeat the three actions and the next step.

Use actual product screenshots only after verifying the depicted capabilities. Label fictional examples and staged demo data plainly.

**Presenter framework**

The central message is: a handoff needs an explicit agreement, and the agreement needs observable evidence that work moved.

Explain five questions:

- Who owns the item before and after transfer?
- What information does the receiving team need?
- What observable event means the transfer was accepted?
- What happens when information, ownership, or priority is disputed?
- Which timestamps and quality measures will show whether the change helped?

Suggested transition into the demo:

> “Let’s apply that agreement to one request. We’ll follow it from submission through acceptance and the receiving team’s next action. This is a staged example of the workflow; it does not establish a measured performance improvement.”

**Demo recovery procedure**

- If a step is slow, explain its operational purpose while it loads.
- If there is no useful progress after 30 seconds, the moderator sends the private cue: “Backup.”
- Switch to the prepared recording or screenshots by 60 seconds. Do not repeatedly retry.
- If the environment fails completely, complete the scenario with backup assets.
- At 27:00, the moderator sends: “One minute; finish current step.”
- At 28:00, transition to the worksheet, regardless of demo state.

Presenter recovery line:

> “This environment is taking longer than expected. I’ll use the prepared capture of the same scenario so we can finish the workflow.”

Moderator transition if needed:

> “We’ve seen the main handoff decisions. Let’s move to how you can apply and measure them in your own operation.”

Label recordings and screenshots when shown. Their limitation is reduced live interaction; their benefit is keeping the session useful and on time.

**Moderator script**

Opening:

Poll transition:

> “Which of these creates the most delay in your operation? Choose the closest fit.”

Question period:

> “We’ll start with questions that apply across teams. For account-specific questions, we’ll capture the detail for follow-up.”

Closing:

> “The worksheet and resources are available at [link]. Thank you for joining us. Start with one recurring handoff, make responsibility and acceptance explicit, and measure what changes.”

**Prepared answers**

“Can we expect a 30% improvement?”

> “We do not have supporting evidence for that figure in this session. Your result will depend on your workflow and starting point. We recommend establishing a baseline and measuring the pilot before setting an improvement expectation.”

“How do we distinguish faster handoffs from rushed work?”

> “Track a quality measure alongside waiting time, such as first-pass acceptance or rework. Also check end-to-end completion time so the change does not simply move the queue.”

“What if the receiving team will not accept ownership?”

> “Define an exception owner and an escalation rule before launch. Otherwise, the handoff agreement depends on informal negotiation each time.”

“Does the product automatically measure all of this?”

> “That depends on the events recorded in your configuration. We’ll confirm the available timestamps and reporting for your workflow before recommending a measurement approach.”

**Task B — 12-minute executive decision presentation plan**

**Decision sought:** Approve a bounded pilot of one recurring handoff process, with a spending and staff-time cap, agreed measurement rules, and a scheduled decision on whether to stop, revise, or expand.

Because financial data has not been supplied, the deck should request a controlled test and show how value will be established. It should not present an ROI estimate or a financial payback claim.

**Proposed pilot shape:** Six weeks: two weeks to establish the baseline and finalize launch criteria, followed by four weeks of intervention. These are planning assumptions. Confirm sufficient case volume before adopting the schedule.

**Meeting success criteria:**

- Executives understand exactly what they are authorizing.
- Finance can trace each proposed benefit to an observable measure and a valuation rule.
- The pilot has an owner, a cap, launch criteria, stopping rules, and a decision date.
- The meeting ends with a recorded decision and named next actions.

**Timing and deck structure**

| Time | Slide or activity | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:45 | 1. Decision requested | Put the approval ask first. |
| 00:45–02:00 | 2. Problem and uncertainty | Explain the suspected friction and what remains unproven. |
| 02:00–03:15 | 3. Process change | Show exactly what the pilot changes. |
| 03:15–04:45 | 4. Bounded pilot | Establish scope, ownership, effort, and controls. |
| 04:45–06:30 | 5. Evidence and value | Explain how the pilot distinguishes improvement from noise and estimates value. |
| 06:30–08:00 | 6. Gates and approval | Present launch conditions, stopping rules, and the requested authorization. |
| 08:00–11:00 | Executive questions | Prioritize finance, operational disruption, and evidence quality. |
| 11:00–12:00 | Decision | Restate the ask and record the outcome. |

**Slide 1 — Approve a bounded test of [handoff]**

Show:

- Selected handoff: [sending team] → [receiving team].
- Proposed duration: six weeks, subject to volume confirmation.
- Accountable owner: [name].
- Requested cap: [staff hours] and [direct spend].
- Decision date: [date].
- Approval condition: finance and operations agree to the measurement plan and thresholds before intervention begins.

Presenter:

> “We’re asking to test a specific process change within an agreed cap. We have not yet established its financial return. The pilot is designed to determine whether it improves flow, preserves quality, and creates value worth pursuing.”

Deck author: Use a compact decision box. Do not invent budget figures; obtain them before circulating the approval version.

**Slide 2 — The suspected problem is waiting at [handoff]**

Show a simple current workflow, with the suspected delay marked. Separate:

- Verified observations: [documented examples or existing measures].
- Hypothesis: unclear ownership, incomplete inputs, or inconsistent acceptance may cause avoidable waiting.
- Unknowns: frequency, magnitude, root causes, and economic consequence.

Presenter:

> “The working hypothesis is that this handoff introduces avoidable delay. The baseline will establish how often it occurs and how much of it the proposed process can address.”

Deck author: If there is no baseline, use a process diagram and an evidence-status table. Do not use an illustrative chart that could be mistaken for measured results.

**Slide 3 — Make the transfer explicit**

Show a before-and-after diagram with the same start and end points.

Proposed changes:

- One accountable receiving owner.
- A short, agreed information checklist.
- A recorded acceptance event.
- A clear exception and escalation path.

Presenter:

> “The change makes responsibility, required information, and acceptance observable. We’ll apply it to one handoff so we can evaluate the result without redesigning the broader operation.”

Confirm whether these are the actual proposed changes. Remove any element that is outside the authorized process scope.

**Slide 4 — Limit exposure and preserve a comparison**

Show a pilot charter:

| Element | Proposed rule |
|---|---|
| Scope | One recurring handoff, defined teams, and a documented eligible-case population |
| Duration | Two-week baseline; four-week intervention, subject to adequate volume |
| Owner | Operations sponsor with a named day-to-day lead |
| Resources | Approved staff-time and direct-spend caps |
| Comparison | Comparable unchanged stream or staggered rollout, where feasible |
| Tracking | Consistent event definitions and case characteristics across both periods |
| Reversal | Restore the previous process if a stopping rule is reached |

Presenter:

> “The pilot limits operational exposure and preserves a comparison where feasible. We’ll record volume, case mix, staffing changes, and other conditions that could explain the result.”

If no credible comparison is available, state that the before-and-after result will provide weaker evidence of causation. This reduces setup effort but increases the risk of attributing unrelated changes to the pilot.

**Slide 5 — Prove operational improvement, then value it**

Use two connected panels.

**Operational evidence**

Primary measure: elapsed time from the first recorded “ready to transfer” event to the first substantive action by the receiving team.

Apply the same definition before and during the pilot. Define what counts as substantive action, which clock is used, and how weekends, pauses, and incomplete cases are handled.

Report:

- Median and 90th-percentile handoff delay.
- Eligible case counts and unresolved cases.
- Rework or clarification frequency.
- End-to-end completion time.
- Actual staff effort required by the new process.

Where comparison data exists, assess whether the pilot improved more than the unchanged stream. Show the size and uncertainty of the difference. Do not exclude inconvenient cases after seeing the result.

**Economic evidence**

Finance should validate each input:

| Potential value | Required evidence | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Reduced handling or rework | Measured active effort saved per case | Value using an approved labor-cost rate |
| Released capacity | Repeated effort reduction and a feasible use for the capacity | Report as capacity until its use is established |
| Avoided cash expense | A specific expense that can actually be reduced or avoided | Count as cash benefit only with an owner and mechanism |
| Faster service | Demonstrated connection to throughput, service commitments, or another valued outcome | Value only when that connection is supported |
| Pilot and ongoing cost | Training, setup, operation, support, and implementation effort | Include both one-time and recurring costs |

Presenter:

> “Shorter waiting time is an operational result. It is not automatically a labor saving. Finance will distinguish cash savings, released capacity, and service value, then compare supported benefits with the full cost of the change.”

If monetization remains unsupported, report the operational result and the unresolved financial question explicitly.

**Slide 6 — Launch only when the test is decision-ready**

Show three gates:

| Gate | Required evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Before intervention | Adequate case volume; usable timestamps; approved scope, cap, comparison, and success thresholds | Launch or revise the test |
| During intervention | Quality and service guardrails hold; spend and effort remain within cap | Continue or stop |
| At review | Operational effect, uncertainty, attributable value, costs, and implementation burden | Stop, revise, or approve a separately scoped expansion |

Set thresholds before the intervention begins:

- Minimum worthwhile improvement: [threshold].
- Maximum permitted quality or service deterioration: [threshold].
- Required evidence strength or precision: [criterion].
- Maximum pilot effort and spend: [caps].
- Economic threshold for expansion: [finance-approved rule].

Presenter:

> “Approval today authorizes this bounded test. Expansion will require a separate decision using the pilot evidence, implementation cost, and remaining uncertainty.”

**Evidence plan for the appendix**

The deck author should include a one-page measurement specification:

- **Population:** Define eligible work and exclusions before launch.
- **Events:** Specify timestamp sources, event definitions, and responsible data owners.
- **Clock:** Choose calendar or business time consistently.
- **Comparison:** Explain assignment or rollout, case comparability, and known confounders.
- **Incomplete work:** Report unresolved cases and backlog alongside completed-case timing.
- **Quality controls:** Check whether apparent speed creates more rework or delays elsewhere.
- **Volume:** Confirm how many cases are needed to support a useful conclusion.
- **Analysis:** Show baseline, pilot, comparison where available, effect size, and uncertainty.
- **Valuation:** Document input sources, finance approval, and assumptions.
- **Decision record:** Preserve thresholds and scope established before results were known.

A short pilot with few cases may remain inconclusive. Its duration should follow the evidence requirement. Any extension should have its own cap and approval rule.

**Presenter question preparation**

“How much will this save?”

> “We do not yet have a defensible estimate. The pilot measures the relevant effort and service changes, and finance validates their value. The approval request is for the capped test.”

“How will we know the pilot caused the improvement?”

> “We’ll use a comparable unchanged stream or staggered rollout where feasible, preserve consistent measurement definitions, and track case mix and operating conditions. If the comparison is weak, we’ll qualify the conclusion.”

“Why not roll it out immediately?”

> “A pilot takes additional time, but it limits disruption and tests whether the added process effort is worthwhile. Immediate rollout would expose more teams before we know its effect.”

“What if people simply acknowledge work faster?”

> “The primary measure ends at the first substantive receiving-team action. We also track end-to-end completion and rework to detect a cosmetic improvement.”

“What happens if the result is inconclusive?”

> “We’ll identify whether the limitation is insufficient volume, unreliable data, or an effect too small to distinguish. We will not treat an inconclusive result as evidence for expansion.”

**Closing decision language**

> “Please approve the pilot for [scope], led by [owner], within [staff-time cap] and [spend cap], with intervention conditional on the agreed measurement and success criteria. The review will occur on [date].”

The moderator or meeting owner records the decision, conditions, accountable owner, and next deadline. The deck author should supply the six-slide deck, pilot charter, measurement appendix, and decision-record template as one package.
