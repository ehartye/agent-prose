# Assessment of source-2.md

**Verdict: suitable for a teacher-led grade 7 literature lesson about narrator knowledge and evidence.** Its main challenge is deliberate: readers must distinguish what the narrator reports observing from suspicion, memory, and information withheld from the reader. That challenge serves the lesson. Extensive simplification would weaken it.

The discussion should keep several distinctions open:

- In P1, the narrator reports leaving the envelope under the cup, finding it gone, hearing Mara's explanation, and seeing a latched window. These are narrated observations and remembered actions, not access to Mara's thoughts.
- The latched window gives reason to question the wind explanation. It does not prove that the window was latched throughout the interval or that Mara took the envelope. The cup's condition is another clue, not a complete account of what happened.
- “I told her it did not matter” reports what the narrator said. It does not establish how the narrator felt. Not asking Mara to search may suggest distrust or concealment; students should identify that as an inference and support it.
- P2 makes even the smile uncertain. The later narrator knows something about subsequent events, but the reader cannot identify those events or infer that the narrator already knows the culprit.

Keep the passage intact for an initial reading. Then ask students to mark “reported observation,” “someone's claim,” and “uncertain memory,” before writing an inference with a quotation and a limitation. A useful prompt is: “What makes the narrator question Mara's explanation, and what does that evidence still leave unknown?” Invite more than one defensible interpretation. Do not supply “Mara stole it” as the answer.

For students who lose the syntax, reread the cup and window sentences in clauses while leaving their wording visible. If “At twelve” causes confusion, discuss its possible time reference in context; do not turn an unresolved detail into a fact through an unsupported edit. A teacher can also separate the time of the scene from the later time of narration.

The tool estimated Flesch–Kincaid grade 3.6. That low surface-language estimate does not make this a grade 3 comprehension task. It cannot measure withheld information, retrospective memory, ambiguity, or the quality of an evidence-based inference. The grade 7 lesson fit rests on those features and the planned instruction.
