# A fictional sleep survey

P1. Although a six-week survey of students at one school found that 68 percent of those who reported at least eight hours of sleep also reported finishing their homework, compared with 45 percent of those who reported less sleep, the resulting association should not be construed as evidence that additional sleep caused homework completion.

P2. The researchers did not assign sleep schedules. The survey used self-report, meaning that students described their own behavior rather than having it measured directly. Moreover, prior attainment and obligations outside school were not held constant, thereby limiting the extent to which the observed difference can be attributed to sleep itself.

P3. These limitations do not prove that sleep has no effect on homework completion. They mean that this survey alone cannot establish such an effect. Readers should distinguish the pattern described in the responses from a claim about what caused that pattern.
