Ratings describe readiness for this audience: **Ready** = sufficient as written; **Revise** = a localized barrier; **Major support** = missing groundwork likely to block the intended understanding. These are editorial judgments, not student test results.

## Proposed changes

1. **Introduce the model before discussing its limits.** Supply the intended diagram, with a key identifying what its dots and spaces represent, or revise the reference to visible dots so it does not assume an unseen image. The diagram and key need content review; their correctness cannot be assessed from this paragraph.
2. **Explain essential vocabulary in context.** Clarify arrangement as how the particles are placed, properties as features of a material, and representation as a way of showing an idea. Matter and particles need a substantive introduction approved by the content author; substituting easier words alone will not supply that knowledge.
3. **Simplify locally without overstating the model.** For the first sentence, consider: “The model shows matter as particles with space between them.” Retain the distinction between the model's symbols and photographs of actual particles. Avoid replacing particles with “dots” throughout, which would blur that distinction.
4. **Request a supported example for the asserted relationship.** The content author should provide an example showing what “arrangement influences the properties” means, supported by the surrounding lesson. The current passage does not establish a particular mechanism or connect the model to floating and sinking. Do not insert such an explanation on the strength of the students' prior topic alone.
5. **Check the actual learning demand.** After the missing groundwork is supplied, an independent reader should be able to identify what the model represents, explain what its dots mean, and explain why the image is not a photograph. Repeating “particles” or “properties” is not enough to demonstrate understanding.
