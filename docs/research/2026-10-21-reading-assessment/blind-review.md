Their evidence is inspectable: P1's delayed qualification and bundled comparison, P2's defined self-report but unexplained research expressions, and P3's distinction between absence of causal proof and proof of no effect.

## Task 2: 2-J and 2-P

Both assess the scene as literature despite the academic parser metadata. Both support its use in the stated teacher-led grade 7 lesson and treat complex clause relationships as useful for inference rather than automatically defective.

Their evidence includes the latched window, unmoved cup, Mara's wind explanation, the qualified memory of a smile, the reference to later events, and the narrator's decision not to ask Mara for help. ## Rating anchors and output-contract consistency

The numbered reports, 1-P, 2-J, and 3-P, explicitly define their four-point scales. Their task-specific anchors are internally understandable, and the assigned ratings agree with the accompanying evidence.

The support-label reports, 1-J, 2-P, and 3-J, use understandable qualitative judgments such as “Ready,” “Light support,” and “Substantial support.” 3-J additionally uses “Major barrier,” with the missing visual and missing prerequisite explanation making the severity intelligible. The labels are less operationally defined than the numbered scales, but they remain adequate rubric-like judgments in these reports. This is a presentation limitation, not evidence that a suitability verdict or proposed action is wrong.

The three support-label reports consistently use six named domains, evidence/reasoning and action columns, prioritized proposals, and detailed diagnostics. The numbered reports use five task-specific criteria and shorter diagnostic treatment. A single fixed schema is therefore more apparent in the former reports. However, the supplied user tasks do not require that schema, an action column, a particular label set, or a full diagnostic footer. Conformity to an unseen output contract cannot be determined here and should not be counted as a general-quality advantage.
