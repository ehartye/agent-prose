# M0 research findings (2026-10-02)

Distilled from seven discovery scouts. Sources are captured in the maintainer's research notes under topic
`agent-prose writing craft`; the plugin's `craft/references.json` will cite the primary URLs
below directly. "Unverified" marks findings taken from search snippets or secondary reports.

## Cross-cutting conclusions

1. **Thresholds disagree across authorities, so length rules warn and cite.** Sentence length:
   Army AR 25-50 averages ~15 words; Federal Plain Language average 20, max 40; GOV.UK hard
   cap 25; Google tech writing gives no number; ISO 24495-1 gives none on purpose. Spoken
   sentences: 8–16 words (CRS 98-170). Make values configurable per form/register.
2. **Readability grade is reported, never targeted** (research notes: Mac et al. RCT; Plain Language
   Canada). Exception that is a real target: NN/g error-message rubric, grade 7–8.
3. **Passive voice is advisory.** No comprehension cost measured (Millar & Budgell 2019) despite
   bans in AR 25-50 and Orwell.
4. **AI-tell rules are style findings, never authorship verdicts.** Detectors < 80% accurate
   (Weber-Wulff 2023) and biased against non-native writers (Liang 2023); experienced human
   readers detect near-perfectly (Russell et al. 2025) using vocabulary + formality cues.
5. **Structure models are advisory.** Beat sheets homogenize (Suderman); Thompson's four
   ~25–30 min parts contradict Field's 30/60/30 (Bordwell); kishōtenketsu has no conflict;
   Monroe's sequence improves perceived organization but not persuasion (UCF 2000).
6. **Sameness is the LLM failure mode in every creative form**: stock jokes (90% of 1008
   ChatGPT jokes were 25 jokes — Jentzsch & Kersting 2023), complexity variance down 21–50%
   (Sourati 2026), persona drift toward default voice past ~100 turns (Araujo 2026). This
   validates variant-set diversity checks and voice-drift measurement.

## By form

### Game dialogue
- Subtitle/line: ≤ 40 chars per line, ≤ 2 lines (3 exceptional), hand-placed breaks
  (Game Accessibility Guidelines; Xbox XAG 104). Hamilton GDC 2019 writeup says 38 — configurable.
- Speaker label on every speaker change or after a 1–2 min pause (XAG 104); tag off-screen speech.
- Localization: budget ~40% horizontal expansion, more for short strings; no string
  concatenation; descriptive variable names + translator comments (Netflix Games i18n).
- Branching: every choice set needs a fallback; repeatable lines need variation; menu text must
  predict the spoken line (paraphrase problem) (Ink docs; Emily Short conversation design).
- Barks: most specific matching line wins with general fallbacks (Ruskin, Valve GDC 2012);
  least-recently-used rotation (Yarn Spinner saliency default).
- Quests: flag the same exposition restated by multiple speakers (Sasko, CDPR GDC 2023).
- Voice distinctiveness is measurable: character 3-gram bootstrap distance (Sela, Eder et al.
  2023); smaller roles score more distinctive (normalize by role size); canonical playwrights'
  voices separate, lesser ones cluster (Vishnubhotla 2019).

### Instructions and technical docs
- One action per step; imperative verb first; location before action; action then result in
  the same paragraph; `Optional:` prefix; single step = bullet; full-sentence intro
  (Google procedures; Microsoft step-by-step). Microsoft: screen readers may skip `>` menu paths.
- Banned in procedures: please, simply, easy, just, quickly, "please note", exclamation points
  (Google voice and tone).
- Diátaxis: how-to titles "How to X" good, "-ing X" bad, bare noun very bad; don't mix modes.
- Users scan (79% scanned; concise +58%, scannable +47%, objective +27%, combined +124%) —
  NN/g 1997; front-load headings with information-carrying words (NN/g F-pattern).
- Error messages: NN/g 12-guideline rubric, grade 7–8, system accountable, preserve input,
  offer a remedy and help resource.
- Experts want plain language too: grade 10–12, 15–20 words/sentence (NN/g 2017).
- Minimalism ≠ brevity (Carroll); cutting information causes failures (Williams & Farkas);
  each procedure needs goal, prerequisites, actions + system reactions, recovery (van der Meij).

### Academic and professional
- Topic/stress positions: old info first, new info last, subject close to verb, action in the
  verb (Gopen & Swan 1990); characters as subjects, actions as verbs (Williams).
- BLUF + active voice; paragraphs ≤ 10 lines (≤ 7 in letters) (AR 25-50).
- LLM lexicon with weights (Kobak et al. 2025 excess ratios: delves 28×, underscores 10.9×,
  showcasing 10.2×; potential, findings, crucial, pivotal, intricate, meticulously);
  Juzek & Ward 21 focal words; Wikipedia era-tagged list and structural tells (negative
  parallelism "not X but Y", rule of three, avoiding is/are, closing "Overall", "it's important
  to note", promotional tone, vague attribution). Em dash 0–9.1 per 1,000 words by model;
  bans don't fully remove it (Freeburg 2026). LLM text is noun-heavy: nominalizations,
  present participial clauses (Reinhart et al. PNAS 2025).

### Speeches
- Speeches ≤ 20 min (CRS 98-170; Sorensen/Kennedy); TED ≤ 18 min, 5 min can suffice.
- Planning WPM: ~130 speech (CRS), 145 lecture, 169 TED (Wingrove 2017); recall best 170–190
  wpm with lower density at higher rates (Rodero 2016); individuals vary 111–291 wpm
  (Yuan et al. 2006) — WPM is a planning default, not a rule.
- Oral style: more "we", contractions, deliberate redundancy and repetition (Lumen, unverified).
- Contrast ≈ 33% and three-part lists ≈ 13% of collective applause; seven formats ≈ 70%
  (Heritage & Greatbatch 1986, unverified secondary); implicit devices draw 2× the responses of
  explicit requests (Bull & Miskinis 2015).
- Signposting conflict: TED says keep structure invisible, no summary ending; macro-markers
  aid comprehension (Chaudron & Richards 1986). Resolve by form: recorded/TED vs lecture.
- Flag the Mehrabian 7-38-55 / "93% nonverbal" myth (Lapakko 2015).
- Sorensen: paragraph → sentence if possible; prefer one syllable to two; cut "I".
- **Evidence gaps:** toasts/eulogies; large hall vs small room vs recorded adaptation.

### Scripts (TV, stage, YouTube)
- Fountain 1.1 grammar is the parser spec (fountain.io/syntax).
- Feature page ≈ 55 s ±20%; only 18% within ±5% (Follows, 2,520 scripts); studio caps ~120 pp.
- Multi-cam sitcom ~40–58 pp for ~22 min (double-spaced dialogue, CAPS action, lettered
  scenes); single-cam 22–35 pp; network hour: teaser + 4–6 acts with early acts longer than
  late (Espenson); streaming/cable-no-ads: no act markers (WGF). Sources conflict on multi-cam
  page counts (Final Draft 52–58 vs WGF measured 40–45) → per-show profiles.
- Stage: 12-pt Courier, 1.5 in binding margin, 1 in elsewhere, character breakdown page
  (Dramatists Guild); festivals use 1 page ≈ 1 minute.
- Comedy: no quips in serious/emotional passages; jokes from character/premise (Espenson; WGA);
  "funniest word last" is register-dependent (August/Espenson against it outside rim-shot
  formats); jokes-per-page norms are multi-cam-specific; benign violation as calibration
  (McGraw & Warren 2010); surprise does not distinguish humor (Warren et al. 2021).
- YouTube: the 30 s "Intro" retention checkpoint = promise-delivery (YouTube Help); every
  title claim must pay off (2024 clickbait enforcement); satisfaction-weighted watch time
  (YouTube blog 2021); confirm the promise in minute 1, re-engage ~3:00 and ~6:00, never signal
  the ending early (MrBeast memo); slower, story-led editing beat hyper-cutting (MrBeast 2024);
  explainers: state the misconception, then refute it (Muller); title sentiment findings
  conflict (Tafesse 2020 vs Cui 2024).
